How summarisation finds the block

Take the lowest network address and the highest broadcast address. The summary is the longest prefix they have in common; every bit after that differs somewhere in the range.

Exact means the summary holds exactly the inputs and nothing else. If it is not exact, advertising it attracts traffic for the extra space — use the minimal list instead, which is the fewest blocks that cover the inputs and nothing more.

Why VLSM sorts largest first

A block must start on a multiple of its own size. Place a small one first and the next big one has to skip ahead to the next boundary, stranding the space between. Largest first keeps every later block aligned for free.

IPv6 prefixes in a sentence

A /64 is one LAN. An ISP typically hands a site a /48 (65,536 LANs) or /56 (256). The first 64 bits route; the last 64 are the host. The mask table for IPv4 is on the subnet page.
